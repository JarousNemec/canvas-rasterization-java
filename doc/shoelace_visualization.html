Cross product and the shoelace formula

Part 1 shows what a single 2D cross product means. Part 2 adds one cross product per polygon edge and shows how the sum turns into the signed area that decides CW or CCW. Canvas coordinates are used, so the Y axis points down.

1. Cross product of two vectors

Drag the tips of u (first vector) and v (second vector). The parallelogram they span has area |cross|, the triangle is half of it. The color shows the sign, the arc shows the turn from u to v. In the shoelace formula u is Pi and v is Pi+1.

Why a single number

Both vectors lie in the XY plane, so the 3D normal points straight into or out of the screen. Its x and y components are always 0 and the z component is the 2D cross product. With X right and Y down, positive Z points into the screen.

2. Shoelace formula

Each edge Pi → Pi+1 makes a triangle with the origin O. Its signed area is cross(Pi − O, Pi+1 − O) / 2. Step through the edges and watch the colored area: the parts outside the polygon are added once with + and once with −, so they disappear and only the polygon stays. The colored area is the running sum drawn point by point: wherever a + triangle and a − triangle overlap they cancel and the color disappears. After the last edge only the polygon is left, in one color. Move O anywhere: the terms change, the total does not.
